# Page composition

Mount the application shell once around the router outlet. Routes compose normal primitives and components inside Main and contribute optional Aside or Panel content. There are no collection-page or record-page wrapper APIs. The stories here render the shapes the [Recipes](?path=/docs/guidance-recipes--docs) name, step by step.

## A record

The trail is `PageHeader.Lead` with the record's code as its last level, the name is the h1, and one Actions menu sits at the end of the title's row (a destination in it is a link item). Tabs follow when the record has more than one view. On Overview the body is Sections, a description is Prose, and the Details are the shell's Aside: an Inspector group named Details with the state, owner, identifiers and dates as KeyValue rows, the ones a reader changes in place as Editables. The Aside is rendered first in Overview's panel and carries the status as its `summary`: beside the Sections it is the rail, and on a phone a Details disclosure above them, closed, its row showing the status. It belongs to Overview and leaves with it; a tab that is a register starts with its Toolbar and table, with no heading over them, and fills the work area.

## A register

A page that is one collection: the name alone in the PageHeader, then a DataTable with `fill`, which takes the rest of the window; on a narrow screen it folds lower-priority fields into each row's More fields ("+3") instead of scrolling sideways. Its [Toolbar](?path=/docs/patterns-toolbar--docs) is the table's `toolbar`: the search first, the saved views in `views`, the filters, Columns, Settings and the collection's overflow as its children, and the one small primary at the end. The name is a TextLink to the record. A failed refresh keeps the rows under one Alert with Retry.

## A preview beside a register

The eye on a row's first value opens a Shell.Panel named by its `label`, never by the record. Its outer header holds global navigation only: [PreviewNavigation](?path=/docs/patterns-previewnavigation--docs) (previous, next, the position said with the record's name through `recordLabel`, and a real open-in-new-tab link) in Panel.Actions, then Panel.Close. The body starts with an inner PageHeader whose Title is the record's name, an h2 because the body of an untitled panel starts its outline at 2, beside one small primary and a subtle overflow menu; the properties follow, then the record's Sections one level down through a HeadingLevelProvider. Previous and next walk the table's displayed rows (`displayedRows`), across pages, turning the table to the row's page (`showRow`). The register stays mounted underneath, so its search and a draft in the panel survive; Close and Escape return focus to the eye.

## Filling the window

`fill` on a DataTable is the `fill-window` utility with `useFillWindow`: the block measures its own offset from the top of the document and takes the rest of the window above Main's bottom inset, its header sticks to its frame, its rows scroll inside and its pagination sits at the bottom. It is never shorter than its toolbar's row, a header, six rows and the pagination; past that the page scrolls again. The height follows the dynamic viewport, so a phone's browser bar never hides the pagination. A Table's `fill` only takes the rest of a bounded column: to fill the window with one, give its parent the `fill-window` class and set the parent's `--fill-top` to the pixels `useFillWindow(ref)` returns for it. Use it for the register that is the page's one block, including a tab that is a register; a register below other content takes its natural height.

## Focus and the sticky shell

The page keeps what focus or an anchor scrolls into view clear of the sticky banner and top nav: the document's scroll padding is their height and `space.200` at the top, and `space.1000` at the bottom. It is the document's own padding, so a dialog, a sheet, a panel or a list that scrolls inside the page brings a focused row wholly into its own view. Below 30rem of height, where the banner and top nav scroll away, the padding is `space.200`.

## Regions and behavior

- **Main** contains the page header, filters, tabs and work area. Keep these visible controls in the route.
- **Aside** provides supporting context, such as a record's Details. It sits beside Main from the `aside` breakpoint (`dimension.breakpoint.aside`, 75rem: 1200px at the default root size), and from the large breakpoint whenever the side nav leaves Main its minimum width beside it: collapsed, or expanded at its default width. Below the aside breakpoint, wherever it would not sit beside Main (below the large breakpoint, beside a side nav the reader widened past that, beside an open Panel), it is a Details disclosure where the route rendered it in the page, closed until the reader opens it, its row carrying the record's state. From the aside breakpoint with a Panel open, it follows Main until the `wide` breakpoint (110rem, 1760px). The breakpoints are tokens; [Metrics](?path=/docs/tokens-metrics--docs) lists them.
- **Panel** holds a selected record or task. From the large breakpoint (64rem, 1024px) it is a resizable column beside Main that runs the full height of the window under the banner; the top nav stops at its edge. Up to the `panel` breakpoint (80rem, 1280px) an open panel shows the side nav collapsed, so the register stays in view beside it, and closing the panel gives the side nav back as the reader left it. Below the large breakpoint it replaces the visible work area under the top nav while Main stays mounted. Close or Escape returns focus to the opener when it still exists.
- **Sheet** is the modal surface for a workflow that needs focus containment. Compose it with the existing Base UI components.

A route contributes at most one Aside and one Panel. Their portal destinations remain stable across route updates. Route React context travels with the contribution, and unmounting the route removes it. Slots appear after mounting; server rendering does not render portaled region content. The application restores its browser workspace before rendering routes.

## Tabs and accessibility

Keep each tab's primary content inside its TabsContent. Supporting context is a separate labelled landmark and is conditional on the selected tab. Give pages one h1, panels an h2, and contextual regions meaningful names. The PageHeader is not a landmark, so the top nav is the page's one banner. Avoid placing modal semantics on a panel that leaves global navigation available.

The examples test route cleanup, retained context, tab relationships, queue state, preview order, resizing, focus return and the fill. Their example route buttons use a horizontal Scroller, so keyboard focus brings each route into view without widening the page on a phone. Responsive behavior is also checked in the application.

## Extending

Compose a new route from Stack, Inline, Grid, PageHeader and the controls it needs. Add a pattern when a repeated interaction earns one, for example record selection, coordinated filtering or calendar scheduling. Layout ownership stays in Shell.
