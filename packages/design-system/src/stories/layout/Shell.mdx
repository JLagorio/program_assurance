# Shell

**Status: Stable.**

The persistent application frame, inspired by [Atlassian's navigation layout](https://atlassian.design/components/navigation-system/layout/). It owns navigation, page gutters, responsive placement and panel scrolling. Product code supplies navigation links and route content.

<Canvas of={Stories.Frame} />
<Canvas of={Stories.ComposedPanel} />

Mount Shell once in a shared router layout. Place Banner, TopNav, SideNav and Main there. Child routes render Shell.Aside or Shell.Panel to contribute to the frame's stable region slots. Contributions preserve route React context and disappear when their route unmounts. They mount on the client after slot destinations exist. The root is a grid of named areas, so its children are those areas: anything else, such as a dialog, a toast or an alert, portals out of it or goes inside an area, since the grid places a stray child in its first free cell.

Every part renders one element and forwards its native props and its ref to it; AppLogo, Profile, SideNav.Item and Panel.Title take Base UI `render` where the element may change, so Profile is a menu's trigger through `render` and a side nav item is a router link. The SideNav.ToggleButton is a child of TopNav.Start and places itself; TopNav.Search goes in TopNav.Middle and opens the product's [SearchDialog](?path=/docs/patterns-searchdialog--docs); TopNav.End is a named group that renders its children as given, and its TopNav.Item children fold into one More menu when the top nav is narrow (see the end slot below).

Aside contains supporting context. Panel contains dismissible selected-record or task content. With `title` (and `actions`) it renders its built-in header, close and resizer; without them compose Panel.Header, Panel.Back, Panel.Title, Panel.Actions, Panel.Close, Panel.Body and Panel.Splitter yourself, as Composed panel shows, to change the heading's level, the labels or the padding. Panel.onClose is required either way and the Title names the landmark. From the large breakpoint (`dimension.breakpoint.lg`, 64rem: 1024px at the default root size; see [Metrics](?path=/docs/tokens-metrics--docs)) the panel is the last column for the height of the window under the banner: the top nav stops at its edge and the panel's header sits level with it, so opening it narrows everything and covers nothing. A banner spans the whole width, over the panel's column too (With banner and panel). From there to the `panel` breakpoint (`dimension.breakpoint.panel`, 80rem: 1280px) an open panel shows the side nav collapsed, as its icon rail with `collapsedSideNav="icons"` or hidden, so the register stays in view beside the panel; closing the panel gives the side nav back as the reader left it, and the preference `persist` remembers never changes; SideNav's `onCollapse` and `onExpand` are not called for it. A reader who expands the side nav while the panel is open keeps it expanded until the panel closes (Panel beside Main at 1200px). Below the large breakpoint the panel replaces the visible Main area under the top nav while retaining Main's state. Global navigation remains available; use Sheet for modal work.

<Canvas of={Stories.PanelBesideMain} />

Aside sits beside Main from the `aside` breakpoint (`dimension.breakpoint.aside`, 75rem), and from the large breakpoint whenever the side nav leaves Main its minimum width beside it (see Main's minimum width below): collapsed, as the icon rail or hidden, it always does, and expanded at its default width it does. With a panel open it sits beside Main from the `wide` breakpoint (110rem); between the aside and the wide breakpoints it follows Main and starts where Main's content ends. Beside Main its divider runs the page's height, however short its content (Record rail at 1100px).

Below the aside breakpoint, wherever it would not sit beside Main (on a phone, beside a side nav the reader widened, beside an open panel), the Aside is a Details disclosure where the route rendered it in the page, closed until the reader opens it: one row over a hairline that reads "Details" (`heading` changes it) and carries `summary`, the record's state as its status badge. The state is one tap from the top and the body keeps its place. So render the Aside where a record's Details belong on a phone: right after the PageHeader, or first in a tabbed record's Overview panel, rendered only while that tab is selected. Where it is rendered it leaves an empty marker that takes no room (`display: contents`), so it adds no gap to the Stack around it; its content portals into the rail or the disclosure. The row is an h2's button with `aria-expanded`; Enter and Space toggle it, and find-in-page opens it. The content sits one level below, and an Inspector.Group named as the disclosure is its content, with no second title. Inside Main the Aside is a region named by its `label`, since a complementary landmark belongs at the top level. The content moves between the rail and the disclosure without mounting again, so a draft in an Editable survives a resize or a panel opening, and it moves as it is, at once: only the reader's own toggle folds it on the kit's motion. Focus on a control inside stays on it as the display changes, the disclosure opening around it; a control the change replaces (a group's title, an h2 in the rail and an h3 in the disclosure) hands focus to the first control left (Record details at 390px, Record rail beside a wide side nav). When a route replaces one record's page with another's, the next record's Details mount in the document, so what measures itself as it mounts measures in the page (Record change at 390px).

The panel's focus contract holds at every width. When it opens while focus is on the page, on its opener or nowhere, focus moves into it (the labelled `aside`, or the element `initialFocus` names), so Escape and the next keys act in the preview; `initialFocus={false}` leaves focus where it is while Main is still visible beside the panel. When it closes with focus inside, focus returns to the control the reader last used in Main: the opener, or another row's eye pressed while the panel was open; `finalFocus` names another element, or `false` leaves it to the caller. Escape closes the panel unless it comes from a field inside it (a text input, a search field, a textarea, a select or an editable region), where it belongs to the field, so a table's search inside the panel clears its query and the panel stays (Table in a panel); from a tooltip, a menu, a list or a dialog open in the panel, which closes first; from text an input method is still composing; or from a part inside that handled it (`preventDefault`). Where the panel replaces Main, it is the page's region: Main, its h1 and its landmark are out of the page until the panel closes, so the panel's title or `label` says where the reader is. See Panel focus.

`defaultWidth` is the panel's own width while it is open and the reader has not resized a panel: a width they dragged or keyed, which `persist` remembers, outranks it, and it is never remembered itself, so each register's preview opens at its own default (Panel widths). A splitter step or a drag lands at once; the columns ease only as the panel opens and closes. Close and the splitter are named after the panel's `label` ("Close Requirement preview", "Resize Requirement preview"), or "Close details" and "Resize details" without one; their `label` props override that. The header stays in view as the panel scrolls, and the panel's scroll padding follows its measured height, so a control that takes focus never stops under a header that wraps. In a window under 30rem tall the header scrolls away with the content instead (Panel in a short window), and beside the page the panel holds the window's whole height once the banner has scrolled away, as the side nav does (Panel in a short wide window).

Every default name (Banner, Top navigation, Side navigation, Main content, Page context, Details, Actions, Search, More, Switch product, the toggle's two labels, the splitters' and the skip links') is a locale message, so a product translates them once with `LedgerProvider`; a `label` prop overrides one instance. The splitters follow the window-splitter pattern: the arrow keys step 16px, with Shift or Page Up and Page Down 64px, Home goes to the minimum and End to the maximum, and Enter collapses the side nav (as a double-click does). Left and Right move the handle in that physical direction, including right-to-left layouts. Each splitter names the area it resizes (`aria-controls`), and `onResizeStart` and `onResizeEnd` report a drag that moved or a key that changed the width, never a key at the limit. Restored widths retain the reader’s desktop preference: the current desktop layout caps them (see Main's minimum width below), and opening the shell on a phone does not overwrite that preference. On a phone the side nav's overlay is at most 90vw wide.

Main uses document scrolling; Panel scrolls inside the available viewport. A register that is the page fills the rest of the window with DataTable `fill`: Main publishes its bottom inset as `--shell-inset-bottom`, and the layout otherwise owns placement, so content patterns do not add nested page frames. A column of a page's grid that stays in view under the banner and the top nav, a wizard's steps beside its form, is a [StickyRail](?path=/docs/layout-stickyrail--docs). The Shell styles its areas by their `data-slot`, never by a class a product writes, so the grid, the areas' stickiness and the panel's container are its own.

Set `collapsedSideNav="icons"` on Shell to keep a 56px navigation rail when collapsed on desktop. The default, `"hidden"`, hides the side nav, and hovering the toggle flies it out over the page. Supply meaningful icons for top-level items and expandable groups. In icon mode, destination names remain accessible and appear in tooltips on hover or focus; the active destination and profile avatar remain visible. Section headings and nested destinations are visually hidden. Custom SideNav.Header content, such as navigation search, is hidden in the rail and returns when expanded. Activating an expandable group expands the shell and opens that group. Hovering the toggle does not open a flyout in this mode. With the default app mark, the collapsed expand control occupies the mark’s position and appears on hover or keyboard focus; on a touch screen (`(any-pointer: coarse)`, the kit's touch predicate: a tablet in landscape, a touchscreen laptop) it shows at rest in the mark’s place. Otherwise the mark is visible at rest, and the product name keeps its position through collapse and expansion and still leads home. The top nav's start slot eases between the side nav's width and its own on the side nav's curve (Start slot motion). Phone navigation keeps its separate visible toggle. Expanding brings the splitter back with the remembered width; on a phone the side nav is the overlay described under Accessibility. `shellScript` and `shellScriptFor` preserve the compact rail before hydration when collapse state is stored. See [Icon rail](?path=/story/layout-shell-icon-rail--desktop) for keyboard, RTL, phone and first-paint examples.

The current page is SideNav.Item's `isActive` (`aria-current="page"`): the selected fill, text and icon, distinct from hover's neutral tint, with a bar on the row's start edge in `color.background.selected.bold`, 3:1 on the fill and on the side nav's surface in light and dark, so the row is told apart by more than its fill's colour; in forced colours the Highlight fill marks it. A SideNav.Expandable that holds the current page takes `isActive` too: it starts open, and its row takes the current colour and bar wherever the item is out of view, while the group is closed and in the rail. In the rail a `badge` stays in its item's name and a dot on the icon says it is there. A label the side nav's width cuts shows its whole name in a tooltip, and so does the profile's name, with its description when that is cut too; AppLogo's `name` and `secondaryName` show whole the same way, on hover and on keyboard focus, when the start slot cuts them (Current page, Long names).

`Shell.Panel.actions` places route-owned controls immediately before Close in the built-in header. Collection previews compose `Panel.Header`, `Panel.Actions` and `Panel.Close` for a navigation-only outer bar: previous/next through the displayed rows and a native full-record link, and for a nested frame `Panel.Back`, the header's first child. ## Main's minimum width

However the side nav and the panel are sized, Main keeps room for its content: one list column (`dimension.layout.list`) inside its desktop gutters (`space.300` each side), 388px with the default tokens. Each area is capped at half the viewport, and also where Main would fall under that minimum. The splitters stop there, and `aria-valuemax` says where, so End is the widest the area can be now. A remembered or default width that no longer fits is narrowed by the layout, not rewritten: when the panel opens beside a wide side nav, the panel's column gives way first, down to its own 240px minimum, and the side nav is capped so that the three always fit without a sideways scroll. The panel's column opens and closes on the standard curve.

## Page changes

Give Shell the router's location as `locationKey` (a pathname or a route id) and it handles a client-side page change at every width, without importing a router: it closes the phone overlay or the desktop flyout, moves focus to Main without scrolling, and announces the page's title through the [Announcer](?path=/docs/components-announcer--docs) it mounts. Focus stays where it is when it is on a control in the page that survived the change, such as a pager or a next-record link. Focus in a menu or a dialog outside the shell, such as a command palette, follows the popup's own return: when it hands focus back to its trigger in the top nav, or drops it, the shell takes it on to Main. The title is `document.title`, read once the new page has rendered, else Main's first h1; `getPageTitle` chooses other words or returns null to say nothing. Pass a key that changes with the page and not with a tab or a filter that writes to the URL, or a tab click would move focus to Main. Without `locationKey` the shell does none of this.

```tsx
const { pathname } = useLocation();
<Shell locationKey={pathname}>…</Shell>;
```

<Canvas of={Stories.PageChange} />

On a phone, choosing a link in the side nav closes the overlay and moves focus to Main even without `locationKey`, so the reader lands on the page they chose; a click that opens a new tab or window leaves it open. Set `closeOnNavigate={false}` on SideNav for links that change the page in place and should keep the overlay open; a change of `locationKey` still closes it. Any other close from inside the overlay (its Close, `useSideNav().collapse()`, Escape, the blanket) returns focus to the toggle once the rest of the shell accepts input again. SideNav's `onCollapse` and `onExpand` receive the `trigger` and `isOverlay`, true for the phone overlay, and fire only when the side nav's state changes: a close caused by a page change has the trigger `"navigation"`, and one caused by the window growing past the large breakpoint `"viewport"`. Keep a remembered collapsed state from the calls whose `isOverlay` is false. `useSideNav().isOverlay` says whether the side nav is the overlay now, so a product never repeats the breakpoint.

## Accessibility

Each area is a landmark with a locale default name: the Banner a region ("Banner"), TopNav a `header` ("Top navigation"), SideNav a `nav` ("Side navigation"), Main a `main` ("Main content"), Aside a complementary region ("Page context"; a region inside Main while it is the Details disclosure) and Panel a complementary region its title or `label` names. Visually hidden skip links come first in the tab order, one per area in the areas' order, and each moves focus to its area; the side nav's link is there only while the side nav shows beside the page, inline or as the icon rail (Skip links). Shell mounts the kit's TooltipProvider: the first tooltip waits 300ms, and the next opens at once for 300ms after the last closed.

Below the large breakpoint the side nav is a modal overlay. Opening it moves focus to the current page's item, else the first link; Tab and Shift+Tab go round inside it; the Banner, the top nav, Main, Aside, Panel and the skip links are inert until it closes. The overlay covers the toggle that opened it, so its own Close takes the toggle's place. Close, Escape and the blanket close it and return focus to the toggle, and choosing a destination closes it and hands focus to Main. The blanket closes the overlay on a press but is no control: it is hidden from assistive technology and out of the tab order. A menu or a dialog opened from inside the overlay keeps its own Tab and Escape, and closing it leaves the overlay open (Overlay focus at 390px).

<Canvas of={Stories.OverlayFocusPhone} />

Profile's avatar is decoration beside the name: Profile hides it from assistive technology, so the button is named by the person's `name` and `description`, and the Avatar inside needs no name of its own. Its chevron says it opens a menu: it shows while the button is a menu's trigger (`aria-haspopup="menu"`, which a DropdownMenuTrigger sets through `render`), and `indicator` decides otherwise. A Profile that opens a dialog says so with `aria-haspopup="dialog"` and has no chevron.

The Banner wraps its message on a narrow screen, and the shell measures the banner area, so the top nav, the side nav and the panel sit under its real height (Banner at 390px). In a window under 30rem tall (a phone on its side, a page zoomed to 400%) the banner and the top nav scroll away with the page instead of pinning a share of the height (Short window).

## Persistence

`persist` remembers, in this browser, the reader's collapsed state and the widths they dragged or keyed on a splitter; a SideNav's or a Panel's `defaultWidth` is the width until the reader resizes, and is never stored, so a changed default reaches every reader who has not resized (Default widths). What an open panel shows between the large and the panel breakpoints is not the reader's choice and is not stored either. `persist={true}` uses one key, `ledger.shell`, for every Shell on the origin, so two frames on one origin with different `collapsedSideNav` (an icon rail in one, a hidden side nav in the other) share one collapsed state and one pair of widths; give the second a key of its own (`persist="ledger.shell.inspector"`) and its head script `shellScriptFor` that key.

### The end slot at narrow widths

`TopNav.End` never shrinks, so what it holds decides the top nav's width. Give each action or destination as a `TopNav.Item` (an icon, a `label`, and `onClick`, or `href` or a router link through `render` for a destination). When the top nav is narrower than 48rem, measured on the row itself so that a panel open beside it counts, the items leave the row for one More menu at End's end: the same icons and names, in the row's order, a destination still a link and an item with a `disabledReason` still saying why. Anything in End that is not an item stays in the row at every width, and so does an item marked `persistent`: keep the colour mode's ModeSwitch as a plain child, since a toggle group cannot become a menu row. An item that opens its own menu or popover is `persistent` and is the trigger through the trigger's `render` (`<DropdownMenuTrigger render={<Shell.TopNav.Item persistent … />} />`), since its popup anchors to the button in the row; an item forwards its native props and its ref to that button, so the trigger's wiring reaches it. Narrowed while an item has focus, focus moves to More, which now holds it, never to a persistent item beside it; widened while More has focus, it moves back to the first item that left. Budget the persistent children for the narrowest window the product serves.

The fold reads the top nav's own width because the top nav is a size container (`container-type: inline-size`, named `topnav`). Inside Shell its width comes from the grid; a top nav placed outside a Shell needs a width from its parent, as a block or a stretched flex item, because it no longer takes one from its contents.

`overflow` is the older, all-or-nothing spelling: below the `md` breakpoint one menu of the product's stands in for every child, the colour mode included (the Record rail at 390px story). Items inside an End with `overflow` never fold; `overflow` still decides.

### The search

`TopNav.Search` is the top nav's way into the product's [SearchDialog](?path=/docs/patterns-searchdialog--docs). In a top nav at least 48rem wide it is a field-shaped button that reads its `label` and shows the shortcut's keys; narrower, a Search icon button with the label in its tooltip, so the middle slot never squeezes a field under the create action. Both are one button to assistive technology, named by `label`, with `aria-haspopup="dialog"` and `aria-keyshortcuts`, and both call `onOpen`. So does the shortcut, ⌘K on Apple platforms and Ctrl+K elsewhere (`shortcut`, `null` for none), from anywhere on the page; it ignores a held key's repeats, other modifiers and text still being composed, and does nothing while a dialog is open, so it never stacks a second dialog over a draft. The search is a button, not a text field: there is one search surface, the dialog, and nothing typed is lost when the row changes width. When the row narrows while the field has focus, focus moves to the icon. One key opens one dialog: `useCommandPalette` also binds ⌘K, and its shortcut cannot be changed, so a page with both gives TopNav.Search another `shortcut`, or `shortcut={null}`.

```tsx
const [open, setOpen] = useState(false);
<Shell.TopNav.Middle>
  <Shell.TopNav.Search label="Search records" onOpen={() => setOpen(true)} />
</Shell.TopNav.Middle>;
<SearchDialog open={open} onOpenChange={setOpen} results={records} onSelect={openRecord} />;
```

The [Search at 320px](?path=/story/layout-shell--narrow-search) story shows the row at a phone's width: the toggle, the switcher, the mark, Search, Create and More in one row.

### Narrow panel headers

Panel header slots respond to the panel container, including a manually resized desktop panel. Below 400px, a title with actions occupies a full row and navigation/Close share the row beneath; title-only headers keep Close beside the title. A PageHeader inside the panel follows its own rule: its actions take the row under the title when the row cannot hold the title's measure beside them. The normal desktop header remains one row. See the 240px, 320px and 640px Panel header stories, composed-slot forwarding and Panel record header examples.
