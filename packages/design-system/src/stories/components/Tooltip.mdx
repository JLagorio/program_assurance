# Tooltip

**Status: Stable.**

A short visual label on hover or keyboard focus, using shadcn's Base UI composition. IconButton already draws one from its `label`; compose a separate tooltip only when its words differ, such as a keyboard shortcut.

```tsx
<Tooltip>
  <TooltipTrigger
    aria-keyshortcuts="Meta+K Control+K"
    render={<IconButton label="Search" icon={<Search />} isTooltipDisabled />}
  />
  <TooltipContent describe>
    Search <Kbd>K</Kbd>
  </TooltipContent>
</Tooltip>
```

## When to use it

- **The name of an icon-only control**, which IconButton draws itself from its `label`.
- **A keyboard shortcut**, or a few words that add to a control's name.
- **The whole of a value cut short**, which Truncate draws itself.

Not for anything the reader needs to finish the task: an instruction, a rule, an error or a required value sits on the page, in a Field's description, a FieldError or an Alert, since a tooltip shows only to a pointer that rests or a focus that lands. Not for a control or a link: a tooltip holds text, a record's preview is a [HoverCard](?path=/docs/components-hovercard--docs) and a small task a [Popover](?path=/docs/components-popover--docs). Not wrapped around a disabled control to explain it: the reason is the control's `disabledReason`, which keeps it in the Tab order and draws its own tooltip (Unavailable actions). [Overlays](?path=/docs/components-overlays--docs) compares them all.

A rule the reader needs is the Field's description, not a tooltip beside the field: a reader who never rests on the icon never meets it.

## Content

- A few words in sentence case, with no full stop: the control's name or a short phrase ("Copy link", "Search").
- It says what the control does, not how to work the pointer: "Delete row", never "Click to delete".
- It adds to the visible label or names an icon; it never repeats a visible label word for word.

## Composition

Root owns open state. Trigger renders a button by default; `render` composes an existing button, link or span with its native props, handlers and ref. It does not give a rendered link or span button semantics, and has no `nativeButton` prop. A custom trigger must accept merged props and its ref, and brings its own focus ring: compose a kit Button, IconButton or TextLink rather than a bare element.

Content includes the portal, positioner and decorative arrow. Native props, `ref`, `render`, and state callbacks for `className` and `style` target the popup. Placement defaults to top and centre with `sideOffset={4}` and `alignOffset={0}`. Content also takes the positioner's `anchor` (an element, a ref or a virtual point, for a tooltip that belongs to a cell or a chart mark rather than to its trigger), `collisionPadding`, `collisionBoundary`, `collisionAvoidance`, `sticky` and `positionMethod`. Logical sides follow the Ledger locale or Content's explicit `dir`.

The surface is Ledger's bold neutral fill with inverse text in `font.body.small`, at most 320px wide (`dimension.part.tooltip`) within the available space; `style` overrides the width. The arrow is 8px with the small radius. In forced colours, where the fill and the shadow are replaced, the popup keeps a CanvasText outline. Content stays inside an enclosing dialog's portal boundary, so it remains visible and hoverable there. It sits on `layer.tooltip`, the top layer, above a toast, so a toast action's label shows over it.

## Timing and interaction

TooltipProvider shares hover timing across a group: the first tooltip opens after `delay`, 300ms, and a neighbour opens at once for `timeout`, 300ms, after the last one closed. Shell mounts one, so every tooltip in an application shares it; a product without a Shell mounts one near its root. Outside any provider, Base UI waits 600ms and groups nothing.

Keyboard focus opens a tooltip at once, without the hover delay. Trigger accepts `delay`, `closeDelay` and `closeOnClick`; a Trigger's own `delay` overrides the provider's. Moving the pointer into the popup keeps it open; leaving both, moving focus away, clicking the trigger or pressing Escape dismisses it. Trigger clicks close it by default; `closeOnClick={false}` keeps it. A tap runs the trigger's action without opening a hover tooltip.

Use Root's `defaultOpen`, or controlled `open` with `onOpenChange(open, details)`. `details.cancel()` rejects a state change. Generic payloads let several triggers share one Root and provide content through its children function.

## Accessibility

- **A tooltip is visual only by default.** Base UI adds no `role="tooltip"` and no `aria-describedby`, so the trigger needs its own accessible name, and what the reader must know stays visible on the page.
- **`describe` says its words too.** On TooltipContent it renders a visually hidden copy that is always on the page and makes it the trigger's accessible description, so a screen reader hears the shortcut or the qualifier the name leaves out. Use it when the tooltip adds words to the name; a tooltip that only repeats the name needs no `describe`.
- **Name a shortcut with `aria-keyshortcuts`** on the trigger ("Meta+K Control+K"), as well as showing it with Kbd.
- **Keep controls and links out of the popup**; content the reader interacts with belongs in a Popover.
- **Inside a Dialog**, Escape closes an open tooltip first and keeps focus on its trigger; the next Escape can dismiss the dialog.

## Unavailable actions

Trigger's `disabled` stops the tooltip; it does not disable the rendered button. To explain an action that truly cannot run, give the Button or IconButton `disabledReason`: the button is disabled with `aria-disabled` but stays in the Tab order, the reason shows in a tooltip on hover, on keyboard focus and on a tap, and it becomes the button's accessible description. Do not wrap a disabled button in a focusable span, and do not put the reason in a `title`, which a disabled button never shows. Prefer an enabled action that explains itself on use, such as a submit that reports what is missing.
