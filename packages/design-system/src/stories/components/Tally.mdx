# Tally

**Status: Experimental.** New with the program's Cyber resiliency view, its first screen; the props may change while a second screen tries it. Owned by the design system.

How many of a group's items are in each state, as one thin bar: each segment its share of a total, the rest the empty track. It is a [TileMap](?path=/docs/patterns-tilemap--docs)'s marks counted into a bar, so it takes the same tones and fills and one key explains both. Put the number beside it: the bar shows the proportion, and the number is the reading.

## Choosing between the bars

| What the reader is watching                                            | Part                                                                             |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A task that finishes: an upload, an import                             | [Progress](?path=/docs/components-progress--docs)                                |
| Parts of a whole in the status tones, a hatched hole, parts that click | `ProgressStacked`, on the [Progress](?path=/docs/components-progress--docs) page |
| Where work stands along a few named phases                             | [PhaseBar](?path=/docs/components-phasebar--docs)                                |
| How many items of a group are in each state, in a map's colours        | `Tally`                                                                          |
| Every item as its own mark, to find one by where it stands             | [TileMap](?path=/docs/patterns-tilemap--docs)                                    |

A tally is not a progressbar: nothing is under way, and a screen reader would hear a percentage of a job that is not running. It keeps apart from `ProgressStacked` by its vocabulary. A stacked bar speaks the status tones (success, warning, danger) and hatches what is unknown; a tally speaks a map's chart tones and has a hollow segment, for what was there and is not now, and a remainder that is the track. Use the one whose key is already on the page.

## Tones and fills

A segment has a `tone`, one of the chart tokens, and a `fill`: `solid`, `outline` for an item that was there and is not now, and `empty`, the track's own colour, for a caller that names the rest as a segment. They are the names a TileMap mark takes (`TallyTone` is `TileMapTone`, `TallyFill` is `TileMapFill`), so one map of a screen's states feeds a `TileMap.Key`, a tally's segments and a [Ladder](?path=/docs/patterns-ladder--docs)'s marks. Keep to a few states: one hue for what changed and a hollow segment for what left read in every colour vision.

<Canvas of={Stories.TonesAndFills} />

Colour never says a state alone. Each segment's `label` ("70 added") is its title under the pointer and a part of the tally's name, and a key beside the tally says what each colour means.

## Sizes

`size` is the bar's thickness: `small` 4px under a name, `medium` 6px in a row, `large` 8px in a band, and `xlarge` 12px where the bar is a table cell's whole reading. The bar is as long as its box.

<Canvas of={Stories.Sizes} />

## Comparing a column

`scale`, from 0 to 1, sets the bar's own length as a share of its box: a row's total against the largest in its column. The bars of a column then compare twice, by their length (how much stands behind each row) and by how full they are (how much of it is held).

<Canvas of={Stories.Scaled} />

## Nothing held

`tone="danger"` is for a tally that holds nothing where something is expected. The unfilled track becomes a hollow outline in the danger tone, never a fill, so an empty bar does not pass for a low value. Say it in the number beside the bar too.

<Canvas of={Stories.Nothing} />

## In a table

In a cell the number is the text and the tally is decoration under it: leave `label` unsaid, so a screen reader reads the row as numbers and is not told of an image a cell.

<Canvas of={Stories.InATable} />

## A changing value

When a value changes, its segment eases to the new share in place over `motion.duration.moderate`. A segment of nothing keeps its place at no width, so it grows from where it stands. Under reduced motion the bar switches.

<Canvas of={Stories.Changing} />

## Accessibility

- **Named or decorative, never between.** With `label` the tally is `role="img"`, named by the label and the labels of its segments that hold something: "Controls behind Adaptive Response: 4 in baseline, 4 added". Without `label` it is hidden from assistive technology, and the number beside it carries the value.
- **Not colour alone.** A segment says its state in its `label`; hollow and filled differ by shape as well as hue.
- **Forced colours.** Filled, hollow and empty stay three things in the system palette: a filled segment is CanvasText, a hollow one an outline, and the track an outline in GrayText.
- **Right to left.** The segments run from the inline start, so the bar mirrors.

## Style

| Element | Token                                                                                                 |
| ------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bar     | `space.050`, `space.075`, `space.100` or `space.150` thick · `radius.xsmall`                          |
| Track   | `color.chart.track`; with `tone="danger"`, a `color.border.danger` outline                            |
| Segment | `color.chart.*` in its tone · hollow: `elevation.surface.current` inside an edge in the tone          |
| Motion  | `motion.duration.moderate`, `motion.easing.standard` on a segment's length; none under reduced motion |
