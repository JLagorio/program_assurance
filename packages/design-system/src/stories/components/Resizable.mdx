# Resizable

**Status: Stable.**

User-sized panes following [shadcn’s Resizable source](https://ui.shadcn.com/docs/components/base/resizable), built on react-resizable-panels. A plain dividing rule is a Separator; the application shell's side nav and panel have their own splitters.

## When to use it

- **Two or three panes whose share the reader decides**: a list beside its detail, a tree beside the record it opens, a source beside its preview.

Not for a fixed line between content: that is a [Separator](?path=/docs/components-separator--docs). Not for the application's side nav or panel: [Shell](?path=/docs/layout-shell--docs) has its own splitters. Not side by side in a narrow container, a phone or a narrow panel: stack the panes with `orientation="vertical"`, or show one at a time (Narrow containers).

## Content

- Name each handle for what it resizes: `aria-label="Resize the list"`. The default, "Resize", tells several splits apart by nothing.
- A button that folds a pane says what it will do next: "Hide the tree", then "Show the tree" (Without dragging).

## Anatomy

```tsx
import { ResizableHandle, ResizablePanel, ResizablePanelGroup } from "@ledger/design-system";

<ResizablePanelGroup>
  <ResizablePanel defaultSize="30%" minSize="20%">
    List
  </ResizablePanel>
  <ResizableHandle aria-label="Resize the list" withHandle />
  <ResizablePanel>Detail</ResizablePanel>
</ResizablePanelGroup>;
```

Give the group a sized parent. Panels clip their contents; put ScrollArea inside a scrolling pane. `withHandle` adds a visible grip.

<Canvas of={Stories.Panes} />

Sizes are strings for percentages (`"30%"`) and numbers for pixels. A share of the split is written `defaultSize="30%"`, never `defaultSize={30}`, which opens a pane 30 pixels wide.

## Variants and states

`orientation="vertical"` stacks panes. Native panel props include minimum and maximum sizes, collapse behavior and resize callbacks. The library owns layout, pointer resizing and keyboard behavior.

## Without dragging

A split that only a drag can change fails a reader who cannot drag (WCAG 2.5.7). The keyboard has the arrows, Home, End and Enter. Give a pointer a way that needs no drag as well: a button that folds and unfolds the pane through its `panelRef`'s `collapse()` and `expand()`, or buttons for the sizes the task needs. Every product split needs one.

<Canvas of={Stories.WithoutDragging} />

## Persisting and driving a split

Persist a layout with `useResizableLayout` (the upstream `useDefaultLayout`) from the package root: give the group a stable `id` and the panels stable `id`s, and pass the hook's `defaultLayout` and `onLayoutChanged` to the group. Keep an existing id to read its saved layout. Supply server-safe `storage` when the hook renders on the server; the story does. Drive a split with the group's `groupRef` (`ResizablePanelGroupHandle`) and a panel's `panelRef` (`ResizablePanelHandle`); `ResizableLayout` and `ResizableLayoutStorage` type what the hook saves. Nothing is imported from react-resizable-panels directly.

## Narrow containers

Panels keep their minimum sizes, so a group needs the width of its minimums; below that it overflows its parent. In a narrow container (a phone, a narrow panel) do not split side by side: stack the panes with `orientation="vertical"`, or show one pane at a time and let the reader move between them. Sizes in percentages follow the container; pixel minimums do not.

## Right to left

In a right-to-left page the first panel is drawn on the right, and the arrow keys move the handle the way they point: ArrowLeft moves it left. Set the direction before the group mounts; a group whose direction changes while mounted keeps the order it measured, so give it a `key` that changes with the direction.

<Canvas of={Stories.RightToLeft} />

## Accessibility

The handle is a focusable separator with the orientation of the rule: a side-by-side group has a vertical separator. Arrow keys resize, Home and End reach the bounds, Enter toggles a collapsible panel and F6 moves between handles. Name each handle with `aria-label` ("Resize the list"), especially when several splits are present; the default is "Resize". On a touch screen the handle takes a 24px hit area. Group and Panel expose native `elementRef`, `groupRef` and `panelRef` instead of translating them into a generic ref.
