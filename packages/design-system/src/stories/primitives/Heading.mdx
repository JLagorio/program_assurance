# Heading

**Status: Stable.**

A title. The size is the title it matches and the level is the page's: `size` picks one of the titles the kit draws (the page and record title, an overlay's title, a section's title) or a figure bigger than a Stat's, with an element to go with it, and `as` overrides the element when the outline needs another level. The kit draws its own titles as Headings at these sizes: PageHeader.Title, Section.Title, DialogTitle, SheetTitle, AlertDialogTitle, DrawerTitle and a Stat's figure. A title set with Heading therefore matches the screen around it. The colour is the text colour, or its inverse on a bold fill, which a Box sets for you; a tone is not a heading colour. Inside a `HeadingLevelProvider` the level comes from the context, as below.

## Anatomy

| Part    | What it is                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Element | The contextual level inside a HeadingLevelProvider; otherwise by size: `page` an `h1`, `section` an `h2`, `overlay` an `h3`, `display` a `div`. `as` overrides either with a `HeadingElement`: a level, a `div` or a `span`. |
| Size    | `page`, `overlay`, `section`, `display`: the four `font.heading` tokens of the same names.                                                                                                                                   |
| Colour  | `color.text`, inherited; `color.text.inverse` or `color.text.warning.inverse` from a bold Box.                                                                                                                               |

## Roles

| Heading   | The title it matches                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| --------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `page`    | The page and record title, the one `h1`: what PageHeader.Title draws on a page, and as a preview's record name at the level its outline gives it. A Stat's figure is this size as a `div`.                                                    |
| `overlay` | An overlay's title: what DialogTitle, SheetTitle, AlertDialogTitle and DrawerTitle draw, each its overlay's `h2`. A group heading inside an overlay takes it, an `h3` there; a card's title is the same style, and CardTitle draws it itself. |
| `section` | A section's title: what Section.Title draws, an `h2` under the page's title and one level below inside a titled Section. A step's heading in a wizard and a rail's group heading take it at their level.                                      |
| `display` | A figure bigger than a Stat's, or the one title of a screen with nothing else on it (sign-in) given `as="h1"`. A `div` by default and in every context, because a number is not a title. Never a page or record title.                        |

Not built, and why: a size between `display` and `page` (the page title is the biggest title the product sets, and nothing in it is a hero); a size for the Eyebrow (it is uppercase and subtle, `font.heading.xxsmall` on the Typography page, and not a title); a separate size for a Stat's figure (it is the page title's size, so the two stay level); a subtitle slot (the line under a title is a Text, and the PageHeader draws it); an icon before (a Heading is words; the mark beside a record's title is the PageHeader's); a tone (a red heading says the word is the problem; the count beside it is).

## Sizes

| Size      | Token                  | Size / leading / weight | Element | Drawn by                                                       |
| --------- | ---------------------- | ----------------------- | ------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `page`    | `font.heading.page`    | 20 / 26 / semibold      | `h1`    | PageHeader.Title; a Stat's figure, as a `div`.                 |
| `overlay` | `font.heading.overlay` | 15 / 22 / medium        | `h3`    | DialogTitle, SheetTitle, AlertDialogTitle, DrawerTitle (`h2`). |
| `section` | `font.heading.section` | 13 / 18 / semibold      | `h2`    | Section.Title.                                                 |
| `display` | `font.heading.display` | 28 / 34 / semibold      | `div`   | A figure bigger than a Stat's.                                 |

`section` is the body size at semibold, the size of a disclosure's header, so a section's title sits level with the controls in its row. Under the ramp, `font.heading.xxsmall` at 11 / 14, uppercase, is the Eyebrow.

## The titles the kit draws

PageHeader.Title, Section.Title, the overlays' titles and a Stat's figure render a Heading at their size, so changing a size on the ramp changes every title drawn at it, and a Heading beside one of them is the same type. A part that renders as another through `render` keeps its own size: a PageHeader.Title rendered as SheetTitle, a preview's record name, stays at `page` as the sheet's `h2`.

<Canvas of={Stories.KitTitles} />

A screen built from those parts needs no Heading for them. Heading is for a title those parts do not draw: a step's heading in a wizard, a group inside a dialog, the title of a screen that has no PageHeader. A raw `h1`–`h6` with a type class copies a title's look and misses the next change to it; write the Heading at the size it matches.

## Level from context

`HeadingLevelProvider` sets which level a heading placed inside takes. With `level` it starts an outline (a portalled region, a rail that sits under the page's h1 at 2); without it, it goes one below the surrounding level, and the first one gives 2, the level under the page's title. `useHeadingLevel()` reads it, `undefined` outside every provider.

The kit's own titles read the same context, and each keeps its default where nothing sets one. On a page without a provider, PageHeader.Title is an h1, Section.Title an h2, EmptyTitle an h2, Shell.Panel.Title an h2, CollapsibleHeader, AccordionTrigger, Inspector.Group, Item.Group and Related an h3. An overlay's title is its `h2` whatever the context, and headings inside the overlay start at 3. A titled Section gives its content the next level, and a Shell.Panel starts at 2 with its body one below a title. A Heading without `as` takes the contextual level too; `display`, a displayed number, stays a div.

<Canvas of={Stories.LevelFromContext} />

Context follows the React tree, portals included: content portalled into another region (a panel, a dialog) keeps the level of the place it was rendered from. A portal that starts a new outline wraps its content in `HeadingLevelProvider level={n}`.

## Deprecated sizes

`large`, `medium`, `small` and `xsmall` are deprecated for one version, each drawn with the step that replaced it. `large` is `display`, `medium` is `page` and `xsmall` is `overlay`, each keeping its element, and `ledger/no-deprecated-name` writes the new names. `small` draws `page` at medium weight, 20 / 26 medium, as an h2: a page or record title is `page`, and a section's title is `section`.

## Modifiers

- **`size`**: required. The title it matches.
- **`as`**: the page's choice, over the context's. `h2` for a rail's group at the section size; `h4` for a subsection; `div` for a title-sized line where the outline already has its `h1`.
- **`color`**: `color.text`, `color.text.inverse`, `color.text.warning.inverse`. Left off, it follows the surface.
- **`id`**, for a section's `aria-labelledby`.
- **No `style`.** A heading has no computed dimension.

## Content

- **One `h1`.** The page's title; every other title is `as="h2"` or below, whatever its size.
- **The outline in order.** No `h3` under an `h1` without an `h2` between, and no level chosen for its size.
- **A title is a noun phrase**, sentence case, no full stop: "Assessment results", "Schedule assessment".
- **The size is the title's job.** A page or record title is `page`, a section's title `section`, an overlay's `overlay`; a title is never set bigger to stand out.
- **`display` is display**, not a page or record title: a figure bigger than a Stat's, or the one title of a screen that has nothing else (sign-in), given `as="h1"`.
- **A tone goes on the fact beside the title**, never on the title.

## Style

| Element | Token                                                                                                         |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Size    | `font.heading.page` · `.overlay` · `.section` · `.display`, family, size, leading, weight and tracking in one |
| Colour  | `color.text` · `color.text.inverse` · `color.text.warning.inverse`                                            |

## Accessibility

- **The level is the element**, and the element is the page's decision, through the context or `as`. The size says nothing to a screen reader.
- **A section is named by its heading.** `<Box as="section" aria-labelledby>` pointing at the Heading's `id`.
- **The outline is a map.** A screen reader's user jumps by heading; two `h1`s are two pages, a skipped level is a missing floor.
- **`display` is a `div`** so a displayed number is not a heading in the outline. A Stat's label is its heading, if it needs one.
- **Contrast is the token's.** `color.text` on every surface and `color.text.inverse` on every bold fill are measured on the Color sheet.
