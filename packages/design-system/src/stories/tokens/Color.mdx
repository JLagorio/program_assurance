# Colour and elevation

Semantic colour is tier 1. Every token references a palette step and carries a light value and a dark value, so a component never knows which mode it is in. Utilities are generated per property: a text token is only reachable as `text-*`, a background as `bg-*`, an icon as `icon-*`, a border as `border-*`. A `default` segment drops out of the name: `color.text.default` in the source is `color.text` on the sheet and `text-default` in a class.

The ramps are on the Palette sheet (Tokens/Palette). No ramp step has a utility. A component reads a semantic token or nothing.

## One accent

The canvas is white: `elevation.surface` is `color.neutral.0` in light. Blue is the only accent. Brand, selected, information and focus all draw on the blue ramp, so blue is spent on action, selection, focus and links and on nothing else.

| Job                                                          | Token                                            | Utility                              |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| The primary button                                           | `color.background.brand.bold`                    | `bg-brand-bold`                      |
| An active row, a checked menu item, an open dropdown trigger | `color.background.selected`                      | `bg-selected`                        |
| A selected tab's text and underline                          | `color.text.selected`, `color.border.selected`   | `text-selected`, `border-selected`   |
| The focus ring                                               | `color.border.focused` at `border.width.focused` | `border-focused`, `border-w-focused` |
| A link                                                       | `color.text.brand`                               | `text-brand`, or TextLink            |

Ids and counts stay neutral, and so does an avatar by default. An Id is blue only inside a link or a button (`ledger/id-not-blue`). Colour that carries no meaning is the accent family: `color.background.accent.*` in six hues, `subtler` under `color.text.accent.*` and `bolder` under `color.text.inverse`, for an avatar's tint, fill or gradient and, later, a tag. Teal and purple reach only the accent family and `color.chart.*`; a status is never drawn in them.

## Text and icons

| Job                                                        | Text                  | Icon                  |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------- | --------------------- |
| Content, sentence-case headings, buttons                   | `color.text`          | `color.icon`          |
| Secondary: navigation, field labels, subtle links          | `color.text.subtle`   | `color.icon.subtle`   |
| Tertiary: metadata, breadcrumbs, placeholders, helper text | `color.text.subtlest` | `color.icon.subtlest` |
| Off                                                        | `color.text.disabled` | `color.icon.disabled` |
| On a bold fill                                             | `color.text.inverse`  | `color.icon.inverse`  |

An icon mirrors its text, one step bolder where a thin glyph needs it. Text and Heading take these as `color="color.text.subtle"`; a class says `text-subtle`.

## Status

A tone is one decision. The subtle fill takes the tone's text. The bold fill takes inverse text; warning's bold fill is light, so it takes `color.text.warning.inverse`. A dot takes the tone's icon colour.

| Tone          | Subtle fill                    | Text                     | Bold fill                           | Icon                     |
| ------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------ | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| `neutral`     | `color.background.neutral`     | `color.text.subtle`      | `color.background.neutral.bold`     | `color.icon.subtlest`    |
| `information` | `color.background.information` | `color.text.information` | `color.background.information.bold` | `color.icon.information` |
| `success`     | `color.background.success`     | `color.text.success`     | `color.background.success.bold`     | `color.icon.success`     |
| `warning`     | `color.background.warning`     | `color.text.warning`     | `color.background.warning.bold`     | `color.icon.warning`     |
| `danger`      | `color.background.danger`      | `color.text.danger`      | `color.background.danger.bold`      | `color.icon.danger`      |

Reach these through the `tone` prop on Badge, Dot, Indicator, Alert, Progress and Stat, never by hand. The `subtler` and `subtle` emphases are for lozenges and non-interactive chips. `hovered` and `pressed` are their own tokens, never alpha on the default (`ledger/no-alpha-token`). What each tone means is on [Badge](?path=/docs/components-badge--docs); a product keeps its values, their words and their tones in one status map that follows those meanings.

## Minted, not yet drawn

The matrix is complete on purpose: every fill has `hovered` and `pressed`, every tone has `subtler`, `subtle`, the default and `bold`, the inverse subtle fill exists for a control on a bold fill, and the bolder text colours exist for a word on a tone's label. About forty of them have no part that draws them yet: a token is minted when its place in the grammar is clear and drawn when the part arrives, so a part never invents a colour. The families still open, and the part each waits for:

| Family                                                                                                       | Waits for                                                                                                                                              |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `color.background.{danger,warning,success,information}.{hovered,pressed}` and `.subtler.{hovered,pressed}`   | A status fill that is pressed: a toned chip that filters, a status pill that opens its record.                                                         |
| `color.background.{warning,success,information}.bold.{hovered,pressed}`, `neutral.bold.{hovered,pressed}`    | A bold button in a tone other than brand and danger.                                                                                                   |
| `color.background.brand.boldest` and states, `brand.subtlest.{hovered,pressed}`, `selected.bold` and states  | A pressed primary, a hovered brand tint, a bold selected control.                                                                                      |
| `color.background.danger.subtler`, `warning.subtler`, the three `.subtle`                                    | A lozenge emphasis between the subtle fill and the bold one.                                                                                           |
| `color.background.inverse.subtle`                                                                            | The resting fill of a control on a bold surface; its `hovered` and `pressed` are drawn by the Banner's action.                                         |
| `color.text.{danger,warning,success,information}.bolder`, `color.border.warning`, `color.border.information` | A word on a tone's label; a 3:1 status border in those tones.                                                                                          |
| `color.icon.warning.inverse`, `color.border.inverse`                                                         | An icon and a border on a bold warning fill; the Box paints its text inverse and icons follow it, so the icon token is reached through `currentColor`. |
| `elevation.surface.pressed`, `elevation.surface.overlay.{hovered,pressed}`, `elevation.shadow.overflow`      | A pressed page surface, a hovered overlay, a scroll edge.                                                                                              |

`color.background.input.pressed` is drawn by every field on focus, and `color.background.inverse.subtle.hovered` and `.pressed` by the Banner's action under the pointer. The chart tokens are reached at runtime through `tone`, which a name scan cannot see; Patterns/Chart.

## Borders

Every border is a 1px hairline: `border.width` is 1px and `color.border` is the default colour. Prefer a rule to a box.

| Job                             | Token                   | Utility                             |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Hairlines, dividers, flat cards | `color.border`          | `border-default`                    |
| Emphasis that must pass 3:1     | `color.border.bold`     | `border-bold`                       |
| Text fields                     | `color.border.input`    | `border-input`                      |
| Checkbox and radio boxes, 3:1   | `color.border.bold`     | `border-bold`                       |
| Focus ring, 2px                 | `color.border.focused`  | `border-focused border-w-focused`   |
| Selected tab or item, 2px       | `color.border.selected` | `border-selected border-w-selected` |
| Off                             | `color.border.disabled` | `border-disabled`                   |

The text-field border is light by decision: `color.border.input` is 1.87:1 on the light page and about 2:1 on the dark one, and the contrast test holds it at 1.5:1 or more on the page, sunken, raised, overlay and input surfaces, including the hovered and pressed input fills. The input surface, the hover tint and the focus and danger borders, which do meet 3:1, carry the affordance. A choice control has no input surface and no text to carry it, so the Checkbox and Radio box is drawn in `color.border.bold` and the Switch's off track in `color.background.input.track`, each 3:1 on every surface. A reader who asks for more contrast gets a text-field boundary of 3:1 or more on every one of those surfaces too: see Increased contrast below. Decorative rules and disabled controls use separate tokens; a decorative border must not be the only visible boundary of an interactive control.

The automated contrast contract covers default, hovered and pressed text/fill pairs, selected rows (their subtle text and icons at rest, under the pointer and pressed; their meta text in `color.text.subtlest`, an Absent included, and the name link in `color.text.brand` at rest and under the pointer, a press being momentary), a highlighted menu item's count or meta line, status fills, focus and input borders, the focus ring on the fills a focused control sits inside, the accent pairs, the chart's marks, and translucent fills on their actual parent surfaces in both modes, at the standard contrast and the increased one. Alpha blends are composed in encoded sRGB before calculating WCAG luminance. These token checks complement rendered interaction and manual accessibility checks; they do not establish conformance for arbitrary custom combinations.

A status border comes in `default` (3:1) and `subtle` (decorative): `color.border.danger`, `color.border.danger.subtle`, and the same for warning, success and information.

## Increased contrast

The standard values above are the default, low-contrast choices included: a 1.87:1 field border, a 1.22:1 hairline, and selected, pressed and highlighted fills between 1.05:1 and 1.8:1. A reader who asks the system for more contrast gets a second set of values, in light and in dark, where every boundary and every state reads 3:1 against the surface, and a focus ring reads 3:1 on those states too.

| What turns it on                     | Where                                                                                     |
| ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `prefers-contrast: more`             | The reader's system setting, for the whole document, unless the root pins standard.       |
| `data-contrast-mode="more"`          | Pins increased contrast on that element and everything inside it, usually `html`.         |
| `data-contrast-mode="no-preference"` | Pins the standard contrast, whatever the system says.                                     |
| No attribute                         | Follows the reader's setting, as `data-color-mode` absent follows `prefers-color-scheme`. |

The contrast and the colour mode are independent and nest either way: a dark region inside an increased-contrast page, or an increased-contrast region inside a dark one, takes the nearest of each. The mode switch does not set the contrast yet; a product that offers the choice writes the attribute on `html` itself, and the Storybook's Contrast toolbar does the same.

| Family                                                                                                    | Standard                                                                                                                            | Increased                                                                                                                                      |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Text-field boundary, `color.border.input`                                                                 | 1.87:1 light, about 2:1 dark                                                                                                        | 3:1 or more on every surface and on the hovered and pressed input fills                                                                        |
| Hairline, `color.border`                                                                                  | 1.22:1 light, about 1.3:1 dark                                                                                                      | 3:1 on every surface                                                                                                                           |
| Pressed and highlighted fills, `color.background.neutral` and `.subtle`, rest, hover, pressed             | 1.13:1 to 1.46:1 light, 1.16:1 to 1.78:1 dark                                                                                       | 3:1 against the surface they sit on                                                                                                            |
| Selected fills, `color.background.selected`, rest, hover, pressed                                         | 1.05:1 to 1.22:1 light, 1.23:1 to 1.97:1 dark                                                                                       | 3:1 against the surface                                                                                                                        |
| Text and icons on those fills: `color.text`, `.subtle`, `.selected`; `color.icon`, `.subtle`, `.selected` | 4.5:1 and 3:1 on the standard fills                                                                                                 | `color.text`, the subtle ones and the selected ones step darker in light and lighter in dark, so they keep 4.5:1 and 3:1 on the stronger fills |
| Focus ring, `color.border.focused`                                                                        | 3:1 on the surface and on the neutral and selected fills at rest, hovered and the selected press; 2.8:1 on a neutral press in light | 3:1 on the surface and on every fill above, a press included; darker in light, lighter in dark                                                 |

What it costs: secondary text reads closer to primary text (in dark, `color.text.subtle` is the same step as `color.text`), and a 3:1 fill is a mid tone, so a coloured word (a link, a status) on a hovered or selected row is not promised its 4.5:1 there, nor a coloured boundary (`color.border.selected`, `color.border.danger`) its 3:1 against the fill; each keeps 3:1 against the surface. `color.text.subtlest` steps up too but is held to the surfaces alone, not to the stronger fills, so a placeholder still reads apart from a value. Every pairing on this page is tested at both contrasts: `test/contrast.test.mjs` runs light and dark at each.

Nothing reads the contrast directly: a part uses the tokens and follows. `tokenValue()` returns the value in effect at the element it is given. For Figma and other DTCG tools the package exports `tokens.dtcg.light-contrast.json` and `tokens.dtcg.dark-contrast.json` beside the light and dark documents. Forced colours (Windows High Contrast) is a different mode, where the system replaces the palette; `forced-colors.css` handles it.

## Blanket and skeleton

`color.blanket` (`bg-blanket`) is the scrim under a dialog, a drawer and the open side nav on a small screen. `color.skeleton` (`bg-skeleton`) is the loading fill; `color.skeleton.subtle` is its shimmer.

## Chart

A series on a chart is a `color.chart.*` token: a status tone when the series is a status, `brand` for the one the reader is asked to look at, `neutral` for context, or the categorical set, six hues in a fixed order validated for colour vision, then Other. Two scales exist for a heatmap: `sequential`, one hue from near zero to the most, and `diverging`, red against blue around grey. Every step is the same in both modes, and every series tone has a `hovered` step (one darker in light, one lighter in dark) for the mark under the pointer. `color.chart.warning.bold` is the warning orange on a grey track, a step darker than the series in light: a Progress bar's warning fill, which holds 3:1 on the track over every surface. The parts read these through `tone` on a series; `fill-chart-*`, `stroke-chart-*` and `bg-chart-*` let a bespoke SVG take the same paint. How each is used is on Patterns/Chart.

Every series tone (`brand`, `neutral`, the four statuses), the six categorical hues, Other, and each one's `hovered` step hold 3:1 against every surface a chart sits on, in both modes, so a bar, a line or a slice reads as a mark without its label. The low steps of `sequential` (1 to 3 in light, 1 in dark) and the non-bold `diverging` steps do not: they are heatmap cells, told apart from the surface by the cell's border and read by the value in them, never a mark alone.

## Elevation

Hairline plus soft lift, never heavy. A surface pairs with a shadow; nothing else casts one.

| Sits                                    | Surface                     | Utility              | Shadow                                          |
| --------------------------------------- | --------------------------- | -------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| The page                                | `elevation.surface`         | `bg-surface`         | none                                            |
| A well: the side nav, a grouped list    | `elevation.surface.sunken`  | `bg-surface-sunken`  | none                                            |
| A card                                  | `elevation.surface.raised`  | `bg-surface-raised`  | `elevation.shadow.raised` (`shadow-raised`)     |
| A menu, popover or dialog               | `elevation.surface.overlay` | `bg-surface-overlay` | `elevation.shadow.overlay` (`shadow-overlay`)   |
| Content scrolling under a sticky header |                             |                      | `elevation.shadow.overflow` (`shadow-overflow`) |

Each surface has `hovered` and `pressed` states. In dark, surfaces climb in lightness instead of casting more shadow. These three shadows are the only ones; `ledger/no-kit-shadow` reports any other.

The pairing is a rule: `surface.raised` always with `shadow.raised`, `surface.overlay` always with `shadow.overlay`, and never the other way round; `surface.sunken` casts nothing and never sits on a raised surface; the page casts nothing. A card inside a card is not a deeper elevation, it is a decision to make with a rule or a well instead. `shadow.overflow` is the one shadow without a surface: the edge of content scrolling under a sticky header, which no part draws yet.

`utility.elevation.surface.current` (`bg-surface-current`) is the surface an element sits on. Box sets it when it paints a surface, and so do Card, Related's cards and every overlay's content (Dialog, AlertDialog, Sheet, Drawer, Popover, HoverCard). A part that must match whatever holds it reads it instead of `elevation.surface`: a sticky header, a pinned cell, a masking fade, a scroll arrow, the ring between overlapping avatars, a Stat tile and a Diff, so each paints a card's colour in a card and a dialog's in a dialog, in both modes.

`opacity.disabled` (0.4) is for a disabled element when no disabled token exists. `opacity.loading` (0.2) is for content that is loading. Motion is on its own page (Tokens/Motion).

## Layers

Which surface sits over which is a `layer.*` token, a stacking order. From the bottom: `layer.chrome` (30), the Shell's banner and top nav, sticky over the page; `layer.blanket` (40), the Shell's blanket under the side nav when it opens over the page; `layer.overlay` (50), everything that opens over the page, the side nav and panel as overlays, popovers, menus, listboxes, hover cards, dialogs, sheets and drawers with their own blankets; `layer.toast` (1000), the toast stack; and `layer.tooltip` (1100), above everything, a toast's included. Overlays portal to the body and stack among themselves by document order, so the one opened last is on top and none needs a number of its own. A region's own sticky parts, a table's header or a pinned column, stack inside Main below the chrome. A class sets a layer as `z-overlay` (`z-chrome`, `z-blanket`, `z-toast`, `z-tooltip`), a stylesheet reads it as `var(--ds-layer-overlay)`, and a new stacking need takes one of these rather than a number of its own; the lint reports `z-30`, `z-40`, `z-50` and any other number and names the layer. A part's own stacking inside itself (a sticky header over its rows, a pinned cell over the middle) keeps `z-0`, `z-10` and `z-20`, and an isolated part (the table frame) keeps them from competing with the layers.
