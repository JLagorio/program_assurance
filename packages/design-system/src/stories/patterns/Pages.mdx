# Pages

The two page archetypes and the parts they are assembled from.

**IndexPage** is a header, one filter row, one dense table. The inline detail surface is the [PreviewRail](?path=/docs/patterns-previewrail--docs), opened from the eye in a Table.Id cell; the record itself is never inline.

<Canvas of={Stories.Index} />

**ShowPage** is a [RecordHeader](?path=/docs/patterns-recordheader--docs), one tab strip running full width, then the tab body. The rail is `rail`, passed on the overview tab and nothing on the others: it renders beside the body, under the tab strip, and every other tab is full width and self-contained. The rail column is `dimension.layout.rail` plus its rule. The rail wraps in an `<aside aria-label="Details">`; the Inspector inside it is a plain `div`, not an aside, so a rail is exactly one landmark.

## Rail, sheet, panel

The rail and the sheet preview a row and leave the list in place; neither is the record. The **[rail](?path=/docs/patterns-previewrail--docs)** goes beside an IndexPage table that leaves room for it, opened from the eye on the id. The **[sheet](?path=/docs/patterns-previewsheet--docs)** is the peek: it slides over the page and the nav to about half the screen, goes over a full-width table (a tree, a board) and wherever the preview carries actions, opened from the row. A page uses one or the other, never both.

The **[panel](?path=/docs/patterns-panel--docs)** is neither. It is the shell's area beside the page, under the top nav, rendered when there is something to show: the detail of a selected row beside its table, flush and without a header, gone when the selection clears; or a panel the reader opens, a thread or a form, with a close and a trigger. The record's rail does not go in it. The page keeps its six facts and the rest, every Inspector group, is the ShowPage's `rail` beside the overview tab, under the tab strip, so the strip runs the full width and every other tab is full width. The peek never goes in it either.

## The hover ladder

Three rungs, each one deeper than the last, and a record reads the same on every rung because the same parts draw it.

1. **Hover on an id or a name is a glance.** A HoverCard: facts only, no actions. It opens on focus too. Program and risk ids do this today.
2. **Click is the peek.** The [PreviewSheet](?path=/docs/patterns-previewsheet--docs): the compact header, the facts, and the actions that make sense without leaving. A record opened from inside a peek is the next frame of the same sheet; the back chevron and the browser's back are the same thing when the stack lives in the URL.
3. **The footer link is the record.** The page, where the work is done. The preview never grows into it.

One preview body per record type, at two densities (glance, peek), rendered by the kit's three shells (HoverCard, [PreviewSheet](?path=/docs/patterns-previewsheet--docs), [PreviewRail](?path=/docs/patterns-previewrail--docs)). The glance is a [`Glance`](?path=/docs/patterns-glance--docs); which facts a record type shows is the app's.

## Choosing many

When the question is "which of these many belong on this one" and the many are found by attribute rather than by name, the picker is a [PickerSheet](?path=/docs/patterns-pickersheet--docs), not a Combobox. Frame one chooses: search and filters that stay put, a sortable Table with Table.Selection, a selection that survives search, a footer that counts. Frame two, when the chosen rows need fields of their own, shows them as a compact table with Editable cells and a defaults row applied to all; the back chevron keeps the selection. The kit owns the frame; the caller owns the columns, rows and selection. The spec is `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-02-picker-sheet.md`.
