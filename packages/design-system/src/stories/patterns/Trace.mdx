# Trace

**Status: Experimental.** New with the risk, operational issue, control and evidence records, its first screens; the props may change while they settle. Owned by the design system.

The stages of a chain as columns of records joined by wires: the controls a requirement meets, the evidence that proves it, what was found against that evidence, the risk that rests on it and what is being done. A record page's tables each show one hop from the record. A trace shows every hop at once, so the reader sees where a chain is thin, where it stops and what it reaches. It reads from one record, the `focus`, outward. A collection the reader searches, sorts or edits stays a [DataTable](?path=/docs/patterns-data-table--docs) on the same page.

<Canvas of={Stories.Playground} />

## Stages, records and links

A stage is a named list of records in the caller's order. A record has a `title`, and may carry a `code` before it, a `mark` for its state and one short `meta` line. Colour never says a state alone: the `meta` says it in words, or the mark has a label. With a `link` the record is that link, so a modifier or middle click opens it in a new tab; without one it is text.

A link names two records by their ids. It is drawn from the earlier stage to the later one, whichever end is named first. A link inside one stage is not drawn: a trace is a chain, not a network.

The trace orders each stage's records around the focus so the wires stay apart: a record sits at the middle of the records it is linked to on the side nearer the focus, and one linked to nothing there keeps its place.

## A link past a stage

<Canvas of={Stories.PassingAStage} />

A link between stages that are not neighbours runs through each stage between them in a gap of its own, so its wire never crosses a record. The gap is room and nothing to read.

<Canvas of={Stories.PassingAnEmptyStage} />

A stage with nothing in it is passed the same way: the gap sits under what the stage says, so the wire runs through the stage from one side to the other, whichever way the page reads, and never across its words. A link named from its later record is drawn the same way round.

## A stage with nothing in it

<Canvas of={Stories.EmptyStage} />

Give every stage of the chain, also the ones the focus does not reach, with what its absence means as `empty`: No evidence linked, No risk recorded. A missing stage is a finding, and a trace that drops the column hides it.

## A step that closes a gap

<Canvas of={Stories.EmptyStageAction} />

Where the reader can close a stage's gap from the record the trace is about, give the stage the step as `action`: a small `TextLink` whose words are the step (Link evidence, Map control), or a link-styled `Button` where the step opens over the page. In a stage with nothing in it the step is drawn under `empty`. A stage with nothing wrong in it, such as No risk recorded, has none.

<Canvas of={Stories.ActionBesideRecords} />

A stage that holds part of what it needs carries the step that adds the rest, after its records and their Show more: a control's requirements map some of its objectives, and the stage offers to map the others (Map 14 more objectives). The step is the stage's and not a record: it stays out of the stage's list and count, lights no path, and Tab reaches it after the stage's records and before the next stage's. Its words wrap in the column as a TextLink's do, a link-styled `Button`'s too, so they stay whole in a column as narrow as 128px.

## A path

<Canvas of={Stories.Path} />

Hovering or focusing a record lights the records it leads to and comes from, through any number of links, and dims the rest. It is a reading aid: nothing is shown only on hover.

## Many records

<Canvas of={Stories.Folded} />

A stage shows `limit` records, eight unless said, and folds the rest behind Show more with their wires; the stage's step stays after Show more. The focus is never folded. A trace is for a chain a reader can take in; when a stage regularly holds dozens, show the stage as a table and link to it.

## Narrow

<Canvas of={Stories.Phone} />

The trace measures its own width. While each column has room to read (128px, and 32px between two for the wires) the stages stand side by side; in less they stack in the chain's order and the wires leave.

## The rail

<Canvas of={Stories.Rail} />

`Trace.Rail` (also `TraceRail`), new with the November 2026 UX proposal and used by no product screen yet, is the compact form: the chain around one record as one line of fixed stations, in the chain's order. The trace draws every record and the links between them; the rail says what stands at each station, so it fits under a record's header, in its body or in a table row, and the full trace becomes its detail. Give every station every time (for the assurance chain: Controls, Requirements, Evidence, Assessment findings, Operational issues, Risks, Remediation), so a station's place is its meaning and a gap lines up down a column of rails. A station is never dropped and never reordered: one with nothing in it says so.

A station's `state` says what stands there:

| State   | Dot                                                         | When                               |
| ------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| `held`  | filled, with its `count`, in the `tone` of its worst record | records stand there                |
| `draft` | a dashed ring                                               | only drafts stand there            |
| `gap`   | a dashed ring in the warning colour                         | the record owes one and has none   |
| `none`  | a small hollow mark                                         | nothing recorded, and nothing owed |

`focus` names the record's own station: a smaller dot of its tone inside a ring, its name in the text colour, and the current item. Under each dot, at `medium`, `value` says what stands there, worst first: the records' links, a judgement (Other than satisfied), counts by severity (1 critical, then 1 moderate as its `meta`), milestones reached. Colour never says a tone alone: the words do.

The legs between the dots are derived from the stations at their two ends, so there is nothing to keep in step. A leg is the link between the nearest stations either side that hold anything, and runs through a station with nothing recorded: solid where it holds, dashed where a draft is at one end, and broken in the warning colour where a gap is. With nothing at one end it is a hairline.

## A gap that breaks the line

<Canvas of={Stories.RailGap} />

A gap says what is missing in words: its `value` (No remediation item), which may be the link, or the link-styled Button, that opens its fix; a gap given none says Missing. The line breaks on both sides of it and stays broken back to the nearest station that holds anything.

## Sizes

<Canvas of={Stories.RailSmall} />

`size="small"` sits in a record's body: the names under the dots, at a fixed pitch of `space.1000`, and no words. `size="xsmall"` fits a table row: the dots alone, `space.200` apart, so the stations line up down a column and a gap reads across a register. Where a station's words are not drawn, its `description` is read after its name ("Evidence, No evidence"), else a string `value`, else the state's own words (Missing, Drafts only, Nothing recorded). Give a toned station a `description` that says its tone in words.

<Canvas of={Stories.RailInARow} />

At `medium` the rail measures its own width: in less than 36rem, a phone or a panel, the stations run down the page with the line beside them.

<Canvas of={Stories.RailNarrow} />

## Accessibility

The trace is a group named by `label`. Inside it the stages are an ordered list, each stage a list named by its `label` and count, and each record's description says what it is linked to (Linked to AU-10 Non-repudiation and Signing key rotation log), so the chain reads without the wires. The focus is the current item. The wires are decoration and are hidden from assistive technology.

The rail is an ordered list named by its `label`, a station an item read by its name, its count and its words ("Operational issues, 2, 1 critical, 1 moderate"), the focus the current item. The line and its dots are decoration. Its counts sit on the bold fills with their inverse words, at 4.5:1 in every mode; its dots, rings and legs hold 3:1 against the surface, and a broken leg and a gap's ring are dashed as well as coloured. In forced colours the dots are the system's text colour, the record's ring its highlight.
