Pass four · the organization

Assess once, use everywhere

You said the product is the hierarchy: business lines, their products, each product's configurations, and the components they share. A flight computer is judged once at the top, or in a program and reviewed at the top, and every product and program that uses it inherits the result and is told when it changes.

  • Assess onceA determination is recorded on a component version. A configuration and a product are built from their components, plus what only integration can show.
  • Use everywhereAnyone on that version, unmodified, inherits the result. An inherited row says where it comes from and is not entered again.
  • Found below, reviewed aboveA program's finding on a shared component is offered to its owner, reviewed once, and then shows for every consumer.
  • Changes, both waysA consumer stays on the version it took and reads Behind when a newer one is published. The new version carries forward every determination except on the controls its owner lists as affected. A source sees who uses it, at which version, and who changed it.

What every board follows

Ten rules, enforced on every board

  1. One loud fact a surface. The standing, set at 32px. Nothing else on the surface is that size.
  2. One picture a surface. A path, a ladder, a strip of marks, the chain. A table is never the picture.
  3. One action slot. Header right on a record, the footer bar in a workspace, a dialog's footer. Nowhere else.
  4. Role-true. The slot holds the reader's own next step. When the ball is in someone else's court it says who, and for how long.
  5. Blue is the act. One advance button. Ink marks state and place: the current tab, today's rule.
  6. A signature is its own button. Ink, with the signer's name. Never batched, never in a table row.
  7. Colour is a judgement. Green only for what is signed, published or satisfied. Red late or denied. Amber at risk or unowned.
  8. Say it once. One home per fact. A count in a heading is not repeated in a chip, a legend and a footer.
  9. Absence is silent. No empty tier, no column of dashes, no row of zeros. A missing owner that blocks work is the one exception.
  10. A path, not a page. Every check ends in the verb that fixes it. Every button leads somewhere that is drawn.

Method

How this was made, and what is real

Who drew it. Seven critics checked pass one: a founder, an RMF practitioner, a flow critic, a systems critic, an art director, a writer and a forms designer. Thirteen researchers mined other platforms for patterns. Six designers explored the first screen and four judges scored them. Twenty-nine designers were then set to draw the set, each followed by an art director.

How it was finished. Usage credits ran out three times in the first run, which stopped at 41 boards, 24 of them unreviewed drafts. A second, slower run finished the set with sixteen agents, three at a time: five drew the eight missing boards, eleven took the drafts through an art director’s pass, and the lead looked at every board before it went on the page. All 89 boards are final. Nine research threads stopped in the first run and were not rerun.

Pass three. After the founder's review the lead rewrote the brief around the control work and read a second session's proposal page for what fits. One designer built the shared shell and the page types; five more drew the boards from them, three at a time, and the lead looked at each before it went on the page. The 49 pass-two boards are unchanged.

Pass four. The founder asked for the organization's hierarchy and its libraries, and for assess once, use everywhere. He answered six decisions by question, and the lead wrote the brief from his answers. One designer extended the navigation to the organization scope; five more drew the boards, three at a time.
